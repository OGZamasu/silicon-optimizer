The party trick

Run AI that looks way too big for your Mac.

Some big AIs are a team of specialists, and each word only needs a few of them. The app keeps those in memory and pulls the rest off disk the moment they're wanted. Same answers, a fraction of the memory.

The normal way
19.0 GB
The app's way
5.32 GB

The trade-off is speed: disk is slower than memory, and long prompts feel it most. The app tells you how much slower before you turn it on.

It needs a special build of the llama.cpp engine, which ships inside the app. Point the app at your own engine and it checks first, then hides the option rather than failing halfway.

What it does

No Terminal. No settings maze. No math homework.

Knows what your Mac can handle

It reads your chip, memory and storage, then says what fits before you download. If something doesn't fit, it lists what to change and how much each change saves.

Picks the AI for you

One button ranks every model against your Mac and takes the strongest one that still runs fast. On a 36 GB Mac that's Qwen3.8 27B. Override it whenever you like.

A chat that actually does things

Pick your agent: DeepSeek Harness, OpenAI's Codex, or the Qwen team's Qwen Code — all running on your own models, no accounts. They browse the web, edit files in a folder you pick, and run commands — asking you first. Every model you have, on this Mac or your other machines, is one picker away.

Makes images

FLUX and friends, running locally. You get a yes or no on memory before the render starts — not at 95%.

Speaks, sings, listens

Type a line and hear it back — LuxTTS, Kokoro or Sesame CSM. There's a music model and a sound-effect model too. Whisper and Parakeet turn speech into text, live from your mic.

Makes video

Describe a shot and get a real clip. Or record yourself and drive a portrait with it — the face moves the way yours did.

Any model, not just a menu

Search all of Hugging Face. The app reads a slice of any model's file and gives you a verdict before you download gigabytes.

Stays out of your way

Lives in the menu bar: what's loaded, how fast, memory at a glance. It warns you five minutes before it frees an idle model, and downloads survive bad Wi-Fi.

Be a character, live

Make a character, put your webcam behind it, and send it to OBS as a browser source. Motion tracking follows your face, shoulders and fingers, and speaks VMC — so VSeeFace, VTube Studio and Warudo take it as-is. Record a take and the character performs it.

Turns images into 3D

Drop in a picture, get a mesh — clean geometry in about twenty seconds, or fully textured in minutes. Spin it in the viewer, copy it out as a turning GIF, or open it in Blender or Fusion 360.

Bring your gaming PC into it

Pair a Windows machine with an NVIDIA card and the heavy jobs go there — video, 3D, portrait animation — while your Mac stays free. You watch it work: stage, step, percent, time left.

The fine print

What's solid, and what's still rough.

Every number on this page is measured, not marketing. Same deal here: what's still rough, so you know where you stand.

  • Video clips need a second machine. Making a clip from a prompt runs on a paired Windows PC with an NVIDIA card. Everything else on this page — chat, images, voice, 3D, talking portraits — runs on the Mac alone.
  • Voice, video and 3D download their own engines. Chat and images work out of the box. The first time you open one of the others, it fetches what it needs — a few gigabytes, once, and it tells you before it starts.
  • Speed predictions start from one Mac. Tuned on an M3 Max; other chips use published specs until you run the built-in one-minute speed test, which tunes them to yours.
  • macOS will complain once. The app isn't notarized by Apple yet, so the first launch needs one command to approve it (below). Building from source skips it.
  • The big-model trick needs a special engine. Expert streaming isn't in standard llama.cpp yet. The build inside the app has it; point the app at your own engine and it checks, then hides the option if that build can't do it.
  • MLX support is early. Apple's MLX engine works, but you point the app at a model folder yourself for now.
  • Multi-part downloads are untested. Models split across several files are planned and checked correctly, but that download path hasn't been exercised yet.

Get started

Running in about two minutes.

1 · Easiest: Homebrew

brew tap OGZamasu/tap
brew install --cask silicon-optimizer

2 · Or download the app directly

# macOS blocks apps from new developers once. After dragging
# it to Applications, approve it with:
xattr -d com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/Silicon Optimizer.app"

3 · Or build it yourself

# Needs Xcode 16+ on an Apple Silicon Mac (macOS 14 or later)
git clone https://github.com/OGZamasu/silicon-optimizer
cd silicon-optimizer
Scripts/build-app.sh --release
cp -R "build/Silicon Optimizer.app" /Applications/

4 · Optional: image generation

# Chat and language models work out of the box — the engines ship
# inside the app. Making images needs one extra install:
python3 -m venv ~/.silicon-mlx
~/.silicon-mlx/bin/pip install mflux

5 · Optional: connect Claude or ChatGPT

Scripts/install-mcp.sh --configure-claude

Everything the chat needs ships inside the app — the llama.cpp engine and the Node.js runtime are bundled, checksummed, and listed in the third-party licences file. If you install your own copies the app finds those too, even inside a Python virtual environment; Settings shows what it found and what each one can do.
